3D virtual tour vs. video walkthrough: which sells faster?

They're not the same product. Here's when to spend on a navigable 3D tour and when a cinematic reel does the job better.

Agents often treat "3D tour" and "video walkthrough" as interchangeable. They're not. One lets a buyer navigate a home on their own time; the other sells the feeling of it in 30–45 seconds. Using the wrong one is either wasted spend or a missed opportunity.

What each one actually is

3D virtual tour

A scanned, navigable model of the home — click to move room to room, zoom, and see true spatial relationships. Delivered as an interactive embed on the listing and often MLS-ready. Best for letting serious buyers pre-screen a property before a showing.

Video walkthrough / cinematic reel

A produced, set to music, paced edit — usually 30–90 seconds. It's an emotional asset: it makes the home feel desirable and is built for social, portals, and the top of a listing page. It sells the lifestyle, not the measurements.

Use a 3D virtual tour when…

  • The home is large or unusual in layout — estates, multiplexes, custom builds. Buyers want to understand the flow before driving out.
  • You're pre-qualifying showings — a 3D tour filters out the merely-curious and brings serious, informed buyers to open houses.
  • The buyer pool is remote or relocating — out-of-town and overseas buyers lean on 3D tours heavily.
  • The listing will sit a while — a 3D tour keeps working 24/7 as a self-serve open house.

Use a cinematic reel when…

  • You need attention fast — a reel is built for the scroll: social, portals, email blasts. It earns the click.
  • The home photographs on feeling — light, views, design. Video shows that better than any still or scan.
  • The listing is straightforward — a tidy semi or condo doesn't need a navigable model; a strong reel plus HDR does the work.
  • You're on a budget — a reel is usually cheaper than a full 3D capture and punches above its weight on reach.

The honest answer: most listings want both, in order

For a typical GTA detached, the highest-converting combo is HDR photos + a cinematic reel for reach, then a 3D tour if the home is large, high-value, or has a remote buyer pool. You don't have to decide blindly — at Bricks the reel and 3D tour are separate à la carte add-ons, so you match the media to the property instead of over-buying a bundle.

"A reel gets them to the listing. A 3D tour gets them to the front door. Most GTA homes only need the first; the second earns its keep on the bigger ones."

Cost reality (GTA, 2026)

  • Cinematic reel: $199–$349 add-on, by footprint.
  • 3D virtual tour: $149–$299, phone-scanned and MLS-ready.

Both are modest next to the cost of a price reduction from a stale listing. The mistake isn't spending — it's spending on the wrong one for the property.

Is a 3D tour the same as a floor plan?

No. A floor plan is a flat dimensioned drawing (included on every Bricks shoot). A 3D tour is navigable and spatial but isn't a substitute for a measured plan.

Which helps SEO / portals more?

Listings with both rich media types get more saves and views on major portals; the reel drives initial engagement, the tour extends time-on-listing.

Can I use the reel on social?

Yes — the cinematic reel is formatted for Instagram, TikTok, and Realtor.ca embeds. That's its main job.
